Prompt Cheat Sheet: Writing with AI

AI can draft the message. You still decide what to send.

1 · Prompt cheat sheet2 · Sound like you

The four ingredients

A prompt is just an instruction. The more context you give, the better the writing.

1 · Role

Who is writing?

“You are a friendly office manager…”

2 · Task

What do you need?

“Write a follow-up email to a client…”

3 · Tone

How should it sound?

“Warm and professional, not salesy.”

4 · Constraints

Length, format, include/avoid

“Under 80 words. Suggest a call. No pricing.”

Copy-paste template

Fill in the orange brackets, then paste into your AI tool.

You are [ROLE]. Write [TASK: type of message, to whom, about what]. Tone: [TONE]. Constraints: [LENGTH, FORMAT, what to include, what to avoid]. If I haven't given you a fact, use a [placeholder] instead of making something up.

Vague vs. specific

Vague

Write a follow-up email.

Result: generic, could be from anyone, may invent details.

Specific

Write a warm, brief follow-up to a small business client named Jordan who I met at a networking event last week. We discussed possibly working together on a website project.

Result: right tone, right length, grounded in your context.

Worked examples

Follow up with a new contact

RoleTaskToneConstraints
You are a freelance web designer writing to a potential client. Write a follow-up email to Jordan, who owns a small bakery and whom I met at a networking event last week. Tone: warm and professional, not salesy. Constraints: under 80 words, suggest a 15-minute call, don't mention pricing.

Chase a late delivery

You are a project coordinator. Write a polite but firm email to a vendor whose delivery is two weeks late. Tone: professional, calm, direct. Constraints: under 100 words, ask for a confirmed new delivery date, don't threaten to cancel.

Decline a meeting

Write a friendly message declining a meeting invitation because my calendar is full this week. Offer next week instead. Tone: warm and brief. Constraints: 3 sentences maximum.

Say thank you (with placeholders)

Write a thank-you note to [client name] after our meeting about [project]. Tone: warm and sincere. Constraints: 3 sentences, mention one specific thing from the meeting as [detail].

At work

Ask your boss for time off

Write a short, respectful message to my manager asking for [dates] off. Tone: professional and positive. Constraints: 4 sentences, mention I'll finish [project] before I go, offer to arrange coverage.

Remind someone about a deadline

Write a friendly reminder to a coworker that [task] is due on [date]. Tone: polite, not pushy. Constraints: 2 to 3 sentences, offer to help if they're stuck.

Summarize a meeting for the team

You are a team assistant. Turn these notes into a short follow-up email with three sections: Decisions, Action items (with owner and due date), and Open questions. Constraints: under 150 words, bullet points. If a detail is missing, use [placeholder]. Notes: [paste notes, with names removed]

Ask for a raise or review

Help me write a message to my manager requesting a meeting to discuss my compensation. Tone: confident and professional, not demanding. Constraints: under 100 words, don't state a number yet.

Politely say no to extra work

Write a message declining a new request because I'm already working on [priority task]. Tone: helpful and honest. Constraints: 3 sentences, offer one alternative, such as a later date or another person who could help.

With customers and clients

Reply to an unhappy customer

You are a customer service representative for a small business. Write a reply to a customer who received [problem]. Tone: sincere, calm, solution-focused. Constraints: under 120 words, apologize once, offer [fix], don't blame anyone and don't promise anything I haven't confirmed.

Send a gentle payment reminder

Write a friendly but clear reminder to [client name] that invoice [number] is [X] days overdue. Tone: polite and professional. Constraints: under 80 words, include the due date as [date], offer to answer questions.

Announce a change

Write a short customer announcement that [change, such as new hours or a price update] starts on [date]. Tone: warm and transparent. Constraints: under 100 words, explain why in one sentence, give a contact for questions.

Ask for a review or testimonial

Write a short, friendly message asking a happy client to leave a review of our work. Tone: grateful, low pressure. Constraints: 3 sentences, make it easy by including [link].

Job search and school

Thank an interviewer

Write a thank-you email to an interviewer for the [job title] role. Tone: enthusiastic and professional. Constraints: under 100 words, mention one specific topic we discussed: [topic], and restate my interest.

Ask a teacher or professor for help

Write a respectful email to my instructor asking for an extension on [assignment] because [short reason]. Tone: polite and responsible. Constraints: 4 sentences, propose a new date of [date], thank them for their time.

Request a reference

Write an email asking [name], my former [manager or teacher], to be a reference for [opportunity]. Tone: appreciative and respectful. Constraints: under 120 words, remind them how we worked together on [project], and make it easy to say no.

Everyday life

Complain to a landlord or company

Write a clear, polite complaint about [issue] that started on [date]. Tone: firm but respectful. Constraints: under 150 words, list what happened in order, say what resolution I want, and set a reply-by date of [date].

Text a neighbor or group chat

Write a friendly text to my neighbors about [event or issue, such as a noisy renovation or a block party]. Tone: casual and considerate. Constraints: 3 sentences maximum, no exclamation marks.

Write a condolence or get-well note

Help me write a short, heartfelt note to [name], whose [situation]. Tone: warm and sincere, not over the top. Constraints: 3 sentences, no clichés, offer [specific help]. I'll add a personal memory myself.

Plan and invite

Write a short invitation to [event] on [date] at [place]. Tone: warm and casual. Constraints: under 60 words, include RSVP by [date].

Change the tone

Don't restart. Iterate.

Keep the same chat and give one instruction at a time.

Make it shorter.
Make it more formal.
Make it sound more casual.
Give me three subject line options.
Give me three different versions.
Remove anything that sounds salesy.

Make it sound like you

Here's a sample of how I normally write: "[paste 2–3 of your own sentences]". Rewrite your last draft to match this voice.

What not to share

When in doubt, use a placeholder like [client name], not the real name. Give AI the situation, not the details.

Never paste: client personal data, confidential contracts, private medical or legal details, unreleased financials.

Writing check: before you send

  • Read it out loud. Circle anything that doesn't sound like you.
  • Check every name, date, number, and claim.
  • Does the tone fit my relationship with this person?
  • Did I leave out private or confidential details?
  • Would the reader recognize my voice?
