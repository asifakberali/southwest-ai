Make AI Sound Like You

Turn a few hundred of your own emails into a style guide you can attach to any prompt.

How it works

Four steps. You do the reading once, then reuse the guide forever.

1 · Analyze

Batches of 20–50 emails

AI studies your greetings, tone, sentences, and habits.

2 · Merge

Combine the reports

One style-guide.md under 1,200 words.

3 · Use

Attach it to prompts

“Follow my style guide. Write a follow-up to…”

4 · Test & refine

Compare with real replies

Fix what feels off, update the guide.

Before you start

Batch the emails. Download 100 emails, batch them into groups of 2-5, and analyze each batch separately.

Redact first. Remove client names, phone numbers, account numbers, and anything confidential. Use placeholders.

Use only emails you wrote. Delete quoted replies and signatures. Include a mix: formal, casual, short, long, good news, bad news.

Your emails only. If they belong to someone else, get permission. Check your AI tool's data settings before uploading work email.

Step 1. Analyze each batch

Run this once per batch, in a fresh message, and save each report.

You are a writing-style analyst. Below are [N] emails written by ONE person. Study only the text the person wrote. Ignore quoted replies, forwarded content, and auto-generated signatures. Analyze their writing and report on: 1. Greetings and sign-offs: exact phrases and how they vary by audience (boss, peer, client, stranger). 2. Tone: how it shifts by situation (good news, bad news, requests, follow-ups, apologies). 3. Sentence style: typical length, structure, and rhythm. Contractions? Fragments? Questions? 4. Vocabulary: favorite words and phrases, words they avoid, jargon, filler or hedging phrases ("just", "happy to", "per my last email"). 5. Formatting habits: paragraph length, bullets, bold, emoji, exclamation marks, punctuation quirks. 6. Structure: how they open, how they make a request, how they close, and how they use calls to action. 7. Typical email length by type. 8. Anything distinctive that makes this person recognizable. Rules: - Support every finding with a short verbatim quote from the emails. - Don't invent patterns. If you see something only once or twice, label it "occasional". - Note counts where possible, for example "used 'Thanks!' in 14 of 50 emails". EMAILS: [paste batch]

Step 2. Merge into a style guide

Paste all the batch reports into a new chat, then run this. Save the result as style-guide.md.

Below are style analyses of the same person's emails, produced from separate batches. Merge them into ONE style guide that I can attach to any future prompt so an AI writes emails that sound like this person. Resolve conflicts by favoring patterns that appear in multiple batches. Keep only patterns with evidence. Write it as direct instructions to an AI ("Write in short sentences. Open with..."), not as a description of the person. Use this exact structure, in Markdown: # Writing Style Guide: [Name] ## Quick summary Five bullet points that capture the voice. ## Voice and tone Default tone, plus how it shifts by situation. ## Greetings and sign-offs A table by audience, with exact phrases. ## Sentence and paragraph style Length, rhythm, contractions, punctuation, formatting. ## Signature phrases (use sparingly) Phrases that recur, with how often to use them. ## Words and habits to avoid Phrases, clichés, and tones this person never uses. ## Structure by email type Short templates for: follow-up, request, decline, apology, thank-you, update. ## Example emails Three short sample emails, in this person's voice, written from scratch (not copied). ## Rules for the AI Eight to ten rules, such as "Keep emails under 120 words unless asked" and "Never use exclamation marks with clients". Keep the whole guide under 1,200 words. STYLE ANALYSES: [paste all batch reports]

Step 3. Use your style guide

Attach the file, or paste its contents, then add your task.

Here is my writing style guide. Follow it closely. [paste style-guide.md] Task: Write [type of email] to [recipient]. Context: [facts]. Constraints: [length, what to include or avoid]. If I haven't given you a fact, use a [placeholder].

Step 4. Test and refine

Give the AI an email you already answered and compare.

Using my style guide, write a reply to this email: [paste an email you already answered]. I will compare it with what I actually wrote.

If it's off, use this:

Your draft is [too formal / too long / too salesy]. I would never say "[phrase]". Based on this, suggest 3 changes to my style guide, then rewrite the draft.

Tips

  • Keep the examples diverse. If all 100 emails are to clients, the guide will fail on casual emails.
  • Keep the guide short. AI follows a one-page guide better than a five-page one.
  • Add real samples. Paste 5–8 of your own emails at the end of the guide as “Reference samples”. This often helps more than rules alone.
  • Always read it out loud. The guide gets you close. Your edit makes it yours.
